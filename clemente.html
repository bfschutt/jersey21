Roberto Clemente

August 18, 1934 – December 31, 1972

Right Field · Pittsburgh Pirates · “The Great One”

“Anytime you have an opportunity to make a difference in this world and you don’t, then you are wasting your time on Earth.” — Roberto Clemente

Why #21 starts here

No number carries more weight in this game than 21, and that weight comes from one man. Roberto Clemente wore it for all eighteen of his major league seasons, and turned it into a symbol of excellence on the field and character off it. Any site built around the #21 story has to start with him.

The player

Born in Carolina, Puerto Rico, Clemente signed with the Brooklyn Dodgers organization before being selected by the Pittsburgh Pirates in the 1954 Rule 5 draft. He spent his entire 18-year career with Pittsburgh (1955–1972), and by the time he was done he had redefined what a right fielder could be — a batting champion, a defensive wall, and one of the most feared arms the outfield has ever seen.

He won four National League batting titles, twelve consecutive Gold Glove Awards, and the 1966 NL MVP award. He helped lead the Pirates to World Series championships in 1960 and 1971, and was named World Series MVP in 1971. In the final at-bat of his final regular season game, he became the eleventh player in major league history to reach 3,000 career hits.

The humanitarian

Off the field, Clemente spent his winters running baseball clinics and delivering aid across Latin America and the Caribbean. On December 31, 1972, after a major earthquake devastated Managua, Nicaragua, he personally chartered a cargo plane loaded with relief supplies, worried that earlier shipments were being diverted before reaching the people who needed them. The overloaded aircraft crashed into the Atlantic Ocean off Puerto Rico moments after takeoff. He was 38 years old. His body was never recovered.

Honored ever since

  • The Baseball Hall of Fame waived its standard five-year waiting period and elected Clemente in a special 1973 vote, making him the first Latino and Caribbean-born player inducted.
  • The Pittsburgh Pirates retired his #21 on April 6, 1973 — one of the first numbers retired in franchise history.
  • MLB's annual Commissioner's Award was renamed the Roberto Clemente Award in 1973, given each year to the player who best combines outstanding play with community involvement.
  • PNC Park's right field wall stands 21 feet tall in his honor, and a statue of Clemente stands outside the ballpark's center field gates.
  • Puerto Rico's professional baseball league was renamed the Liga de Béisbol Profesional Roberto Clemente in 2012.
An ongoing movement: unlike Jackie Robinson's #42, which Major League Baseball retired across every team in 1997, #21 has not been retired league-wide. In recent years, players, managers, and fans — including a public call from Houston Astros manager Joe Espada — have pushed for MLB to do the same for Clemente. As of this writing it remains a campaign, not a done deal.
